Aster demo on IST Health

1 October 2026  |  Status: research finished, build paused until the decisions in section 4 are made  |  Detailed research: ASTER_OPERATIONS_RESEARCH.md

This note gives one clear picture of what Aster is, what we would build in IST Health, the choices that change the work, and what we recommend. It is written in business terms; the detailed research is the companion document.

1In one paragraph

Aster is a large Indian hospital group. In 2025-26 it merged with Quality Care India (CARE Hospitals, KIMS Health, Evercare) into Aster DM Quality Care: 39 hospitals, 10,600+ beds, 7,400+ doctors. We studied it from its own public filings and websites and wrote down how it is organised, which services it offers, and how a patient moves through it: outpatient visit, emergency, admission and discharge, surgery, lab and imaging, pharmacy, billing and insurance, and international patients. The next step is to recreate a believable, fully synthetic version of that in IST Health, to show how IST Health would run a hospital group like Aster.

3What "the demo" would be

A new IST Health customer called Aster, filled with made-up people and activity that follow Aster's real proportions: bed counts, occupancy, stay length, payor mix and specialty mix. Nobody in it is real: no real patients, no real doctors, no real prices.

A viewer signs in as different people (front desk, nurse, doctor, lab, radiology, cashier, accountant, hospital administrator, and ideally group leadership) and walks ten scripted patient stories, for example:

Emergency

A chest-pain emergency that ends in a cashless insurance discharge.

Planned surgery

A robotic knee replacement with insurer pre-approval.

Women and children

A delivery, and a premature baby in the newborn ICU.

Long-term care

A monthly cancer-treatment patient on a government scheme; recurring dialysis.

International

An overseas patient on a cardiac surgery package.

Outpatient and diagnostics

A clinic visit with video follow-up, a corporate health-check camp, a home blood test.

What decision 2 means in practice

(A) One customer per hospital what we have now

  • Simple; each hospital's data is fully apart.
  • No view across hospitals.
  • A patient seen at two hospitals is two different people.

(B) One Aster customer, hospitals inside recommended

  • Matches how Aster works: one patient app, one group.
  • Group leaders see all hospitals together.
  • Needs real changes to IST Health.

For (B), the changes are: each user belongs to one or more hospitals and picks one at sign-in; wards, beds and appointments are filtered by hospital; group roles see everything while hospital roles see only their own; and a few group reports are added. We would first check how much of this GNU Health already supports.

What decision 4 means in practice

IST Health stamps every record with the moment it is created. To show 3 months of history we need a different loading method that writes data directly with past dates. It is more work, but the demo then looks like a hospital that has been running for months.

7Risks and things to be careful about

  • Realism vs honesty. The demo is synthetic. Anything shown as a price, doctor or hospital process that Aster never published must be labelled illustrative.
  • Brand use. Using Aster's name needs approval before any outside showing.
  • Scope growth. "The entire group" could mean millions of records. One-tenth scale and three hospitals keeps it manageable.
  • Shared server. The demo lives on the same server as live IST Central. It is isolated, but we should avoid heavy loading during working hours.
  • Multi-hospital support is the main technical risk. Step 2 exists to size it before we commit.
